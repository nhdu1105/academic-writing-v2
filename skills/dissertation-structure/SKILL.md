# Dissertation architecture

A dissertation fails as a piece of writing long before it fails as a piece of research. The most common structural failure is chapters that are individually competent but do not talk to each other: a literature review that identifies a gap the methodology does not address, findings that are never compared back to the studies reviewed, recommendations that do not follow from the findings.

The fix is architectural, and it has to happen before drafting. This skill builds that architecture.

## The golden thread

One sentence must run through the entire dissertation and be visible in every chapter:

```
[Research question] → [gap it fills] → [method chosen to answer it]
→ [findings that answer it] → [conclusions that answer it]
→ [recommendations that follow from it]
```

Every section must be traceable back to that thread. If a section cannot be linked to it, the section is cut - regardless of how well written it is. Before approving any outline, state the thread explicitly at the top and check each proposed section against it.

## Word budget

Total limit 15,000 words, **including** the 1,000-word MBA path reflection. Excluded from the count: cover pages, contents pages, titles and sub-titles, reference lists, appendices. Tables and figures are permitted up to 30% of the limit, but markers penalise obvious use of tables to dodge the count.

| Section | Rubric weight | Target words | Hard max |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive Summary | — | 500 | 700 |
| Ch1 Introduction | 10% | 1,500 | 1,700 |
| Ch2 Literature Review | 20% | 3,000 | 3,300 |
| Ch3 Methodology | 20% | 3,000 | 3,300 |
| Ch4 Findings & Discussion | 20% | 3,500 | 3,800 |
| Ch5 Conclusions & Recommendations | 10% | 1,500 | 1,700 |
| Reflection on Learning | 10% | 1,000 | 1,100 |
| MBA Path Reflection | 10% | 1,000 | **1,000 (stated cap)** |
| **Total** | | **15,000** | |

Chapter 4 carries more words than its 20% weight suggests because it holds both data presentation and discussion. The MBA path reflection cap of 1,000 words is stated explicitly in the brief - treat it as absolute, not as a target with tolerance.

Penalty scale for exceeding the total: 1-10% over carries no penalty; 11-20% costs 10% of the mark; 21-30% costs 20%; beyond 30% the work is capped at a pass. Build the budget at 15,000 and treat the 10% tolerance as emergency headroom, not as planned capacity.

## Required document order

Fixed by the brief. Do not rearrange:

Title page → Executive summary → Acknowledgement → Lists of contents, tables and figures, appendices → Introduction → Literature review → Methodology → Empirical study / findings / discussion → Conclusions and recommendations → Reflection on learning → Footnotes → Glossary (optional) → References → MBA path reflection → Appendices

Title page rules: full title, author as SURNAME (upper case), comma, forename; the qualification statement *"This dissertation is submitted in partial fulfilment of the requirements of Staffordshire University for the award of Master in Business Administration"*; collaborating organisation if applicable; month and year. Note the separate submission rule that no name appears anywhere in the assignment file and the file is named with the student number only - reconcile these with the module leader if they appear to conflict.

Numbering: two-digit for tables and figures, chapter first (Figure 2.3 = third figure in Chapter 2). Appendices use a different sequence (Appendix A, B or I, II).

## Chapter architecture

### Chapter 1 - Introduction (10%)

Function: orient the reader, establish significance, define scope. Moves as a funnel from broad context to the specific research question.

```
1.1 Project background - how the topic arose; the real-world problem
1.2 Significance - why it matters practically and academically
1.3 Research aim - one overall aim
1.4 Research objectives - three to five SMART objectives
1.5 Research question - stated plainly, not buried in prose
1.6 Structure of the dissertation - one paragraph per chapter
```

Objectives must map one-to-one onto the conclusions chapter. This is checked directly by markers.

**The rhetorical logic underneath.** The section list is the container; the persuasion is the content. Swales' CARS model names three moves the introduction must complete, in order:

| Move | Purpose | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Establish the territory** | Show the topic is real, active and consequential. Synthesise prior work by theme, do not list it. | Widest; may take two paragraphs |
| **2. Establish the niche** | Show what is missing, unresolved or contested. This is the pivot of the whole chapter. | Shortest and sharpest |
| **3. Occupy the niche** | State what this study does, in terms that mirror the niche. Purpose sentence, then RQ, then contribution. | Concrete, unhedged about actions |

Three failures to design out:

- **The universal negative.** "No study has examined X" is unverifiable and irritates markers. Scope it instead: "a search of [named databases] covering [period and design scope] identified no studies that..." Or state the limit positively: "evidence in this area remains confined to [design/context]."
- **The niche-purpose mismatch.** If Move 2 identifies a gap about *mechanism* and Move 3 announces a purpose about *prevalence*, the chapter contradicts itself. Markers notice immediately. Draft the purpose sentence first, then check it answers the stated gap exactly.
- **The unlicensed gap.** A gap claim is only as credible as the literature synthesis preceding it. If the sources found do not support the gap as stated, soften the claim rather than assert it.

Hedge findings, not actions. "This study estimates..." is correct; "This study aims to explore..." is weaker and less specific.

### Chapter 2 - Literature Review (20%)

Function: build a critical argument that culminates in an explicit gap statement. It is not a summary of what others wrote.

```
2.1 Theoretical foundation - the core theories the study draws on
2.2 The independent variable - what is known
2.3 The dependent variable - what is known
2.4 The relationship between them - what is established, what is contested
2.5 Mechanisms and boundary conditions - mediators, moderators
2.6 Contradictions, gaps and under-studied contexts
2.7 The gap this study addresses, and why it matters
2.8 Conceptual framework and hypotheses (if applicable)
```

Structural requirements: majority of sources from the last ten years; theory cited from original authors rather than through secondary sources; synthesis organised by theme rather than by paper. The failure mode to design out is the sequence "Smith (2020) found... Jones (2021) found..." - if the outline is organised by author, restructure it by argument.

### Chapter 3 - Methodology (20%)

Function: justify every decision against the alternatives. The word is *justify*, not *describe*. A methodology chapter that explains what was done without explaining why it beat the alternatives cannot reach the upper bands.

If the design has not been chosen yet, or if section 3.10 needs drafting, read `references/methodology-selection.md` first. It covers the six designs viable at this scale with their failure modes, the selection questions in priority order, sample size floors, the human-subjects decision tree, and the specific problem of using data from one's own employer in a regulated sector.

```
3.1 Research philosophy - positivism / interpretivism / pragmatism, and why
3.2 Research approach - deductive or inductive, and why
3.3 Research design - quantitative / qualitative / mixed, justified against alternatives
3.4 Data requirements - what data the objectives demand, and where it exists
3.5 Data collection method - alternatives reviewed, chosen one justified
3.6 Sampling - population, technique, size, and the reasoning behind the size
3.7 Instrument design - construct operationalisation, source of each measure
3.8 Data analysis method - alternatives reviewed, chosen one justified
3.9 Validity and reliability - how measures capture what they claim
3.10 Ethics - informed consent, anonymisation, participant rights, approval status
3.11 Methodological limitations
```

Written in past tense: the dissertation is produced after the research is complete.

### Chapter 4 - Findings and Discussion (20%)

Function: the intellectual contribution is most visible here. Two parts that must not be blurred.

```
4.1 FINDINGS - what the data shows. Factual. Tables, charts, outputs. No interpretation.
4.2 DISCUSSION - what it means, argued against the literature reviewed in Chapter 2
    4.2.1 Answering objective 1
    4.2.2 Answering objective 2  (order the discussion by objective)
    ...
    4.2.n Unexpected findings and their possible explanations
```

The most common failure named in the Dissertation Guide: moving from data presentation straight to conclusions without discussing findings against the literature. Markers call it *describing data without contributing knowledge*. The discussion must name specific studies from Chapter 2 and engage with them - where findings agree, explain why; where they diverge, that divergence is the most valuable part of the dissertation and needs a serious explanation.

Order the presentation to reflect the research objectives, not the order the analysis happened to run.

### Chapter 5 - Conclusions and Recommendations (10%)

Function: answer the question, confirm the objectives, tell an identified audience what to do. No new material.

```
5.1 Direct answer to the research question - stated clearly, not hedged into meaninglessness
5.2 Objective-by-objective review - achieved or not, and if not, why
5.3 Recommendations - for a named audience, traceable to specific findings
5.4 Limitations
5.5 Future research
```

Recommendations must be traceable. Each one states which finding produced it. Unfulfilled objectives are highlighted and explained rather than hidden - the brief says explicitly that they may present opportunities for future research.

### Reflection on Learning (10%)

Personal reflection on the experience of doing the research, structured on Kolb's Learning Cycle (1984): concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualisation, active experimentation. Questions to answer: what was learnt, what was achieved that was not originally intended, what should not have been done, what would be done differently.

### MBA Path Reflection (max 1,000 words)

Must address all five competencies. Missing any one drops the criterion.

| Competency | Focus |
|---|---|
| Strategic Agility | Thinking across time horizons; adapting to new information |
| Organisational Agility | Navigating constraints, pivoting, managing uncertainty |
| Customer Focus | Value delivered to a real audience - practitioners, policymakers, users |
| Drive for Results | Maintaining standards under time and resource pressure |
| Career Ambition | How the dissertation relates to professional direction |

## Producing a chapter-level outline

When asked for a single chapter, produce this and get approval before any drafting begins:

```
## Chapter [n]: [Title]  — target [x] words

Golden thread link: [how this chapter serves the RQ]
Rubric criterion served: [criterion + weight]
Inherits from previous chapter: [what must already be established]
Sets up next chapter: [what this must deliver]

| Section | Words | Argument to be made | Evidence needed | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
```

The "Argument to be made" column is the important one. If a section can only be described in terms of topic ("discusses credit scoring") rather than claim ("argues that credit scoring accuracy gains have not translated into access gains"), the section is not yet designed.

## Structural audit

To check existing work, verify:

- [ ] The RQ appears in Chapter 1, is answered in Chapter 4, and is answered again directly in Chapter 5
- [ ] Every objective in Chapter 1 has a corresponding subsection in Chapter 5
- [ ] The gap stated at the end of Chapter 2 is the gap Chapter 3 is designed to fill
- [ ] Every construct measured in Chapter 3 was defined in Chapter 2
- [ ] Every study engaged with in Chapter 4's discussion was introduced in Chapter 2
- [ ] Every recommendation in Chapter 5 names the finding it derives from
- [ ] Each chapter opens by referencing the central argument and closes by pointing forward
- [ ] Terminology is identical across chapters - one term per construct, no synonyms
- [ ] No finding in one chapter contradicts a claim made in another

Failures here are cheap to fix at outline stage and expensive to fix at 12,000 words.
